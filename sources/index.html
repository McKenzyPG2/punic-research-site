What supports the argument?

A source reading, a model label and a historical interpretation answer different questions. This site keeps their authority and uncertainty separate.

From witness to hypothesis

Physical object → edition → located expression → proposed annotation → interpretation → hypothesis and scholarly review. Matching a quotation verifies its relation to an input, not the edition’s correctness or the interpretation’s truth.

Delos uses a deterministic extractor with in-sample diagnostics against agent-authored provisional references. Lepcis displays selected results from a real LLM experiment, with edition dates supplied and no independent accuracy benchmark. Neither changes canonical claim statuses. All computations here are precomputed; no model, authentication or backend service runs at runtime.

Next steps are specialist collation, independently reviewed annotation guidelines, genre-matched sampling, source and restoration sensitivity analyses, held-out monument evaluation and careful separation of inscription dates from narrated events. A corpus-scale multimodal programme remains proposed.

Delos: digital readings and unresolved chronology

I.Délos 1519; Roussel and Launey, Inscriptions de Délos (1937), no. 1519. PHI digital transcription; Louvre Ma 840. The comparison preserves Greek, line boundaries and editorial signs, followed by derived Latin transliteration and provisional English—not reconstructed pronunciation or a published translation.

Digital witnesses and catalogue material were inspected; printed edition/apparatus and specialist approval remain outstanding. The ancient archon formula is distinct from modern BCE proposals: Habicht 153/2, Kennell 151/0 and Meritt 149/8. Their arguments and inspection limits are retained in the chronology view. No date is adopted.

Short attributed ancient phrases are used for textual comparison, not a source-database republication. Modern editorial or database rights are not presumed extinguished. No source image, full modern translation or scan is included.

Dougga: six words for close criticism

RIL 2 / KAI 101, Massinissa bilingual. Selected Punic expressions follow Donner and Röllig, Kanaanäische und aramäische Inschriften, no. 101, as displayed in Hebrew characters by DEAPS (James D. Moore and Justin Wheaton, record citation). The online edition/year metadata is inconsistent; the physical edition remains unverified. These are not the stone’s Punic letterforms.

The INP Bardo RIL 2 account supplies museum context; its BCE conversion differs from the INP Dougga account. Both are retained. Published office interpretation is discussed in Chaker’s author-hosted text; see bibliography below.

Limited-quotation basis: three two-word expressions are necessary to examine the wording of local collective, royal recipient and office. Each is attributed with its line locator and discussed directly. Their extent is proportionate to this critical purpose; the page does not substitute for the edition or database, and does not reproduce its apparatus, full text, layout or published modern translation. Latin aids and English glosses are project renderings, with provisional English informed by the attributed translation.

DEAPS carries an OSU copyright notice and no explicit open transcription licence was found in its inspected record/About page; INP reserves rights. These notices are respected, not treated as a prohibition on all scholarly quotation or as a licence. The release relies on limited attributed quotation for criticism, not on claiming the modern edition is public domain. This is a documented editorial reuse assessment, not permission from the rights holders or a legal clearance; it grants no blanket redistribution rights.

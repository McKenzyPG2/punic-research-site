Featured case · Seven selected monuments

From Punic institutions to Roman civic identity?

Continuity, transformation and commemoration in the inscriptions of Lepcis Magna.

The question mark matters. These texts record different civic and institutional expressions; they do not demonstrate a population-wide transition in ethnic identity.

IRT 412 · The central distinction

One monument. Two kinds of time.

Date of inscription

202 CE

A civic commemoration of Lucius Septimius Severus, the emperor’s grandfather.

Earlier career

Not independently dated

Sufes, prefect, duumvir and perpetual flamen. The monument recalls these offices; it does not place them in 202 CE.

Why can’t we put these offices at 202 on a timeline?

The kinship term auo in line 6 identifies the grandfather. Line 7 names the local honorand and offices; line 10 continues the career:

du(u)muir(o) fl(amini) p(er)p(etuo)

IRT 412, l. 10 · abbreviation expansions retained. Our provisional translation: “duumvir, perpetual flamen.”

The connecting clause is part of the source context, not one of the model’s selected annotations:

8 publ(ice) creato cum primum ci 9 uitas Romana adacta est

Our provisional translation: “publicly appointed when Roman citizenship was first introduced.” The word ciuitas crosses the line boundary. The precise legal referent and its date require scholarly adjudication. Neither 110 nor 202 CE is assigned here as an office date.

This undated earlier-career panel is schematic, not a measured interval. No office dates have been invented.

Executed model experiment · Selected outputs

What did the model actually do?

One documented run requested gpt-6.1-sol and generated 26 annotations from seven edition-derived records. All 26 quoted spans matched their supplied source lines. Dates and cautionary instructions were supplied in the prompt.

This measures quotation integrity, not independently established historical accuracy or annotation completeness. The four-annotation cap per inscription restricted coverage: IRT 412’s citizenship clause was discussed at record level but omitted as an annotation span.

Model provenance and evaluation scope

Run recorded 17 November 2026; requested model alias gpt-6.1-sol, reasoning low; Codex CLI 0.167.0-alpha.4.1. No immutable backend model snapshot was supplied. These are stored results, not live inference. No specialized training or fine-tuning occurred.

The task required exact single-line quotations, categories, actors, preservation, uncertainty and abstention. It warned about retrospective careers and ethnicity inference. Success is therefore prompt-conditioned. Assistant-assisted checking is not independent expert adjudication; no precision, recall or completeness score is asserted.

Published examples are verbatim selections, not the complete private response. Prompt and output fingerprints are retained in the manifest; raw execution/session records are excluded.

Observations are not yet an identity trajectory

Textual observation: IRT 319, 321 and 347 combine suffete terminology with religious or imperial framing. The first two concern the same benefactor. IRT 301, 353 and 393 record different civic formulations, with restoration especially important in 353. IRT 412 is retrospective.

Historical explanations to test

Institutional transformation; persistence of local offices; Roman civic and imperial incorporation; commemorative conventions. These explanations can overlap.

Alternative source explanations

Different genres, patrons, audiences and selective survival may produce the apparent differences. Seven selected monuments establish no quantified long-term trajectory, prevalence or causal sequence.

Existing scholarship already investigates these problems. Barron (2020) discusses Latinisation of Punic titles; Quinn (2010) addresses the reinvention of Lepcis. Their full arguments and subsequent scholarship need review before a claim of novelty.
